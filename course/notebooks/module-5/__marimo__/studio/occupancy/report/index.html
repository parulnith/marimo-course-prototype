Choosing a threshold for room occupancy

A summary of how well a simple sensor rule detects occupied meeting rooms, and what changing its decision threshold would mean.

Background

Each meeting room reports light and CO₂ readings. A scoring rule combines them into a score between 0 and 1, and a reading counts as occupied when its score is at or above the decision threshold.

The threshold decides which mistake happens more often. A false positive treats an empty room as occupied, so lights and air conditioning may stay on. A false negative treats an occupied room as empty, so lights could switch off on people.

Method and limits

  • The score is a fixed rule (70% light, 30% CO₂), not a trained model or a probability.
  • Results compare predictions with recorded occupancy for the same readings, so they describe this data, not performance on new rooms or dates.
  • Which threshold is best depends on whether wasted energy or lights switching off on people is the bigger cost.
